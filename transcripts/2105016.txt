Ground truth = the known-correct answer you measure your system against.
The key fact for this project: it does not exist anywhere — you create it
by hand, from the images. Your books are scanned photographs with no
reliable digital transcript (that's exactly why Project Gutenberg is
banned as a source: its books are already typed, so there'd be nothing to
read). The only source of truth is a human looking at a scanned page and
writing down what it actually says.

For A2 specifically, the ground truth you need is the OCR evaluation set:
~20-30 held-out pages, hand-transcribed *diplomatically* (Unicode NFC,
keep 1919 orthography, Bengali numerals, and real English/Latin terms as
printed — do not modernize or "fix" anything), human-verified, stored in
grading_kit/labels.jsonl. It must never be trained/tuned on. There are
actually four different ground truths in the project (OCR labels ->
CER; canonical remedy aliases; retrieval gold pages; answer gold+citation
in A3) but only the first is needed now.

[TURN 2 — AI]

Stop and read this first: if Qwen's raw output is going straight into
labels.jsonl, that breaks the measurement. The held-out labels are the
answer key — if a model wrote the answer key, the answer key has the
model's mistakes baked in, and comparing your OCR to it tells you nothing
real. If your OCR makes the same mistakes as Qwen, your CER looks falsely
low; if it makes different mistakes, falsely high. Either way it's
meaningless, and it won't match the grader's own human-checked pages.
Qwen can produce a first *draft* that a human then corrects against the
image — the human correction is what makes it ground truth, not the model
output.

Then, ground-up: data/raw/ is the corpus (the "library"); a scanned page
is a photograph, not text, which is why OCR exists; OCR = a pretrained
model predicting characters from pixel shapes, and Bengali is hard here
because of conjuncts/matras/code-switching. Held-out pages are the exam
answer key for grading OCR, kept unseen so the score is honest, and later
double as the source of A3 test questions. On training: you mostly DON'T
train anything — Tesseract, the BGE-m3 embedder, and the eventual
answering LLM are all pretrained models you *run*, not train. "Reproduce
a pretrained method" means run an existing trained model, which running
Tesseract already satisfies. Labeled data at this stage means evaluation
labels (the held-out set), not training data.

[TURN 3 — AI]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
From your own corpus — every team's books are different, so there's no
universal secret set. Mechanically: grading_kit/manifest.yaml is "the
single entry point a grader opens." It points to your heldout_pages/,
labels.jsonl, and the build/run entrypoints (build_index.sh). The grader
runs YOUR pipeline on YOUR held-out images and recomputes CER against
YOUR labels itself, rather than trusting the number you typed — that's
what makes it "hidden": independently recomputed, not self-reported. It's
possible staff also hold back a separately-transcribed slice of your same
books, but either way the source is always your corpus. Practical
consequences: report real numbers (they'll be recomputed), never train on
held-out pages, keep the pipeline reproducible enough that the grader's
re-run actually succeeds, and keep the labels honestly human-verified.
